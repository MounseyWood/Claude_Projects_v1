Annealed Alphabeta script that argues with itself

Twenty-six glyphs are heated until meaningless, then cooled. Two forces work on them the whole way down: each glyph pushes away from every other so it survives being read badly, and all of them pull toward a shared vocabulary of strokes so they still look like one hand. Everything below is live — the alphabet has not finished deciding.

A typeface is usually described as a set of shapes. It is more useful to think of it as a packing problem: every letter has to claim its own territory in the space of things an eye can resolve, and there are only so many territories. Crowd the alphabet, or blur the reader, and the territories start to overlap — that overlap is the matrix above, and it is the only thing this program is trying to minimise.

Left alone, that objective produces a bag of unrelated marks. So a second term pulls the opposite way, rewarding each glyph for building itself out of strokes its neighbours already use. Distinctness and family resemblance are genuinely opposed, and where they settle is the design. Drag the two sliders against each other and you can watch a script slide from an alphabet into a monogram.
