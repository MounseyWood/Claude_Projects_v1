# The Thread and the Loop

An interactive, phone-first fibre lab for FN4005 undergraduates about polyester and recycling. It is built like a hands-on science-museum gallery: eighteen full-screen exhibits snap like a social feed, and there are no information-only pages. Facts arrive as numbered discovery cards when you do something (26 in all). A wrong move gets a question back instead of the answer.

One Verlet-simulated thread runs through every exhibit and is the controller. It keeps what you do to it, so its shape, filament count, form, wrap and colour carry forward. The care label at the end reads the thread back and decides whether the loop can close. Text blocks are solid: the thread routes round them.

Exhibits:

- **Warm-up:** guess how much fibre is polyester.
- **Make:** link the chain (PET from oil, 1941 Terylene); melt, push and stretch (draw the fibre).
- **Shape:**
  - Pick a nozzle (round, trilobal, hollow or channelled), then test it under the microscope with light, cold or sweat.
  - One strand or hundreds (mono-, multi- and microfilament).
  - Cut it and spin it (staple fibre and twist).
- **Combine:**
  - Wrap it (core-spun with cotton, wool or elastane).
  - Colour it (a dye bath that only works at about 130°C, or pigment into the melt). Blends reveal that only the polyester takes the dye.
- **For and against:** a tug-of-war.
- **Wear:** the washing machine is the interface. Fine, staple and loose yarns shed more.
- **Return:**
  - Where recycled polyester comes from.
  - Feed the recycler: blends jam it; strip them off.
  - Spot the PET in a PET/PLA fork.
  - Stretch without elastane (bicomponent spring).
  - Turn up the heat: the PLA ply melts first.
- **Promise:** tie knots. Then the care label, and tutor notes and sources (learning outcomes, seminar questions, confidence ratings, 23 sources).
