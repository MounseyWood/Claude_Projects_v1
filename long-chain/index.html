Notes and sources

For tutors, and anyone who wants to check the numbers.

By the end, students can

  1. Say what polyester is: PET, mostly from oil, melt-spun into fibre.
  2. Weigh its pros and cons for a particular garment.
  3. Explain why blends, especially with elastane, block recycling, and what "mono-material" means.
  4. Describe two routes to stretch without elastane, and ask what evidence backs each claim.

How it teaches

  • The thread is the controller. Students pull it to draw the fibre, drag it across the grid to guess, fork it, melt it, stretch it and knot it. The idea and the action are the same thing.
  • Predict, then test. Students commit before they see: the 59% guess, spot the PET, will it recycle, which ply melts first.
  • Consequences carry forward. The thread keeps what you do: how far it was drawn, the fraying, any elastane, any PLA, your knots. The care label at the end reads it back.
  • Weigh, don't rank. The tug-of-war has no right answer. It depends on the garment, which is the seminar.
  • Evidence is labelled. Every claim on screen has a source and a confidence rating below.

Seminar questions

  1. Your leggings say "78% recycled polyester, 22% elastane". Where can they go at the end of their life? What would you need to find out?
  2. Play the tug-of-war for a raincoat, a T-shirt and a swimsuit. Why does the knot move differently?
  3. PLA composts only in industrial facilities. Is that a real benefit for a UK wardrobe?
  4. A brand says its stretch fibre is "recyclable". What evidence would you ask for, and from whom?
  5. Redesign one stretch garment you own as a mono-material. What do you lose? What do you gain?

How sure are we?

  • Strong Polyester was 59% of fibre in 2024; 88% fossil-based; under 1% of fibre from recycled textiles [1].
  • Strong Invented 1941 by Whinfield and Dickson [2][3].
  • Moderate Less water than cotton: the direction is agreed, the size varies by study [4].
  • Moderate 6,000,000+ microfibres from one 5 kg wash: one lab study [5].
  • Moderate Elastane in ~80% of discarded US clothes: one estimate [6].
  • Moderate 0.1% PLA spoils PET: bottle recycling, 2009 [7].
  • Weak Two-polyester stretch fibre recycles with polyester: the maker's own tests [8][9].
  • Strong PLA melts ~170°C, PET ~250°C [10]. All-polyester rubbers reach ~1,000% elongation (11× length) in the lab [11].
  • Strong EU textile producer responsibility: in force 17 October 2025 [12].

How the thread works

Each frame's thread is a chain of points. Every step, each point keeps moving the way it was already moving, gravity and a breeze nudge it, and then the links are pulled back to their proper length ten times over. That's Verlet integration. To draw a shape, every point is pulled a little towards a target drawing. Text blocks are walls: a point that strays into one is pushed back out the way it came, and the block gets a nudge.

Think first: what would happen if the links were corrected once per step instead of ten times?
